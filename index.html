Pythagoras

Learn the formula, try a worked example, and test yourself with a 5-question quiz.

What is Pythagoras?

Pythagoras was a Greek mathematician. His big idea was that in a right-angled triangle, the squares of the two shorter sides add up to the square of the longest side.

The longest side is called the hypotenuse, and it is always opposite the right angle.

The formula

a² + b² = c²
  • a and b are the two shorter sides
  • c is the hypotenuse
  • Use this when the triangle has a right angle

Worked example

A right-angled triangle has sides 3 cm and 4 cm. What is the length of the hypotenuse?

Step 1: Use the formula: a² + b² = c²

Step 2: Put in the values: 3² + 4² = c²

Step 3: Work out the squares: 9 + 16 = c²

Step 4: Add them: 25 = c²

Step 5: Find the square root: c = 5

So the hypotenuse is 5 cm.

Common mistakes

  • Forgetting that c is the longest side
  • Using the wrong sides in the formula
  • Forgetting to square the numbers before adding
  • Not taking the square root at the end

Quick practice

1. If a = 5 and b = 12, then c = ?

Answer: 13

2. If a = 6 and b = 8, then c = ?

Answer: 10

3. If a = 7 and b = 24, then c = ?

Answer: 25
